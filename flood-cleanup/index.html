Why Hire a Professional for Flood Cleanup?

Getting the standing water out is only one part of cleaning up after a flood. Chemicals, bacteria, sewage and other contamination ride in with the flood and create real health dangers. A trained cleanup team knows how to set containment, wear PPE (personal protective equipment) correctly and sanitize by protocol, which protects your household from hepatitis, E. coli and other diseases carried in water.

Improper cleanup also tends to miss moisture you cannot see, and within days that trapped water can cause a major mold problem. Professional technicians use moisture meters, thermal imaging and psychrometric calculations to make sure every affected area, including wall cavities, subfloors and framing, reaches an acceptable drying standard. This scientific approach to drying is the only reliable way to prevent secondary damage that can cost thousands more to fix.

How to Tell You Need Flood Cleanup

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Living areas flooded by contaminated water from a backed-up drain or sewer
  • Mud, silt or debris left on floors once the water goes down
  • Water pooled on any floor of the building, no matter where it came from
  • Soaked landscaping that leaves water pooling against your foundation
  • Roof or wall openings left by a storm, with rain pouring through
  • Carpet that squishes underfoot or drywall that feels soft and wet
  • Heavy rainfall, a dead sump pump and water spreading across the basement

Flood Cleanup Prevention Tips

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Add a sump pump with battery backup, since power outages and storms tend to arrive together
  • Install backwater valves on sewer lines to stop sewage from backing up into your home
  • Look into a flood insurance policy, including when your home sits outside a mapped flood zone
  • Check that grading on all sides of the house carries water away from the foundation
  • Use waterproof bins and raised shelving for anything valuable kept at ground level or below
  • Cracks in the foundation should be sealed and the basement walls given a waterproof coating
